How to use this lab

This lab models two samples of liquid water reaching a shared equilibrium temperature in an insulated container.

  1. Set each mass and starting temperature using a slider or number box. A can be warmer or cooler than B.
  2. Before mixing, predict the final temperature and whether it will be above, below, or equal to the simple average.
  3. Press Mix water. The samples approach equilibrium over an illustrative two-second animation. Compare your prediction with the calculated result.
  4. Press Record this run to save the current case, or Load my investigation for the nine-run 80/20 °C mass sweep. Change one mass at a time to investigate the graph.
  5. Export CSV saves the table. Reset assigned case restores 0.20 kg at 80 °C and 0.30 kg at 20 °C, clears predictions, and preserves the table. Clear table removes recorded data.

Q > 0: the sample gains energy. Q < 0: the sample loses energy. Equal and opposite Q values do not require equal temperature changes: ΔT = Qmc.

The readouts show the calculated final changes before mixing. During animation they show transferred energy and temperature changes so far; Tf stays the equilibrium target.

System, assumptions & limitations
  • Isolated two-sample water system: total ΔU = 0 J exactly.
  • Liquid water near 1 atm; constant c = 4184 J/(kg·°C), equal for both samples.
  • Insulated container with negligible heat capacity. No evaporation, heat loss, or phase change.
  • Models the final equilibrium state only, not mixing time. Real evaporation and heat loss would make the measured final temperature lower than predicted.

Model: QA = mA c (Tf − TA); QB = mB c (Tf − TB); QA + QB = 0 J.
Therefore Tf = mA TA + mB TBmA + mB = TB + fA(TA − TB).

The displayed residual is calculated from both Q equations. Floating-point residuals below 0.000001 J are displayed as 0.0 J.
